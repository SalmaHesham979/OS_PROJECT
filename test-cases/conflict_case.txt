============================================================
         TEST SCENARIO: CONFLICT CASE (POLICY CONFLICT)
============================================================
Description: This scenario illustrates the core trade-off: Priority vs Efficiency. 
A long process (P1) has high priority, while a short process (P2) has low priority.

INPUT DATA:
| Process | Arrival Time | Burst Time | Priority |
|---------|--------------|------------|----------|
| P1      | 0            | 10         | 1        |
| P2      | 1            | 2          | 5        |
| P3      | 2            | 1          | 5        |

------------------------------------------------------------
1. PRIORITY PREEMPTIVE RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------
Gantt Chart:
|     P1     |  P2  | P3 |
0           10     12   13

Output Metrics:
| Process | WT | TAT | RT |
|---------|----|-----|----|
| P1      | 0  | 10  | 0  |
| P2      | 9  | 11  | 9  |
| P3      | 10 | 11  | 10 |
|---------|----|-----|----|
| AVERAGE | 6.33| 10.67| 6.33 |

------------------------------------------------------------
2. SRTF RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------
Gantt Chart:
| P1 |  P2  | P3 |     P1     |
0    1     3    4           13

Output Metrics:
| Process | WT | TAT | RT |
|---------|----|-----|----|
| P1      | 3  | 13  | 0  |
| P2      | 0  | 2   | 0  |
| P3      | 1  | 2   | 1  |
|---------|----|-----|----|
| AVERAGE | 1.33| 5.67| 0.33 |

KEY OBSERVATIONS:
1. Priority Choice: Priority scheduling keeps P1 running because it is more "urgent" (Priority 1), forcing shorter jobs to wait.
2. SRTF Choice: SRTF preempts P1 as soon as P2 arrives because P2 is much shorter, significantly improving average waiting time.
3. Trade-off: Priority favors importance; SRTF favors system throughput and speed.

CONCLUSION:
SRTF is more efficient for the overall system (lower Avg WT), but Priority is better if P1 is a critical system task that cannot wait.
